Changing something

Suggest it here, against a component you can point at. Edits land in /css/style.css and arrive on every tool at once — there is no per-tool copy to keep in step.

The one rule

Used by more than one page: style.css, and shown on this page. Used by exactly one page: that page's own file. When a second tool wants it, it moves here in the same commit.

Colour

Eight values, and no more. Six of them are greys doing structural work; the hierarchy is carried by the gap between the bench and the paper, not by tinting things. The two colours that are not grey are each reserved for one job, which is why they still register when they turn up.

--ground
The bench. Page background, everywhere.
--ground-sunk
A recess in the bench: the header band, a hover, an inline code span.
--rule
Every hairline drawn on the bench.
--paper
The artifact. Pure white so it reads brighter than the bench it lies on.
--paper-rule
A hairline drawn on paper rather than on the bench. Lighter, or it reads as printed content.
--ink
Text, and the fill of the one action that matters.
--ink-soft
Labels, units, hints — anything that names a value rather than being one.
--reg
Registration cyan. Focus, hover, trim marks, and a value being adjusted right now. Never decoration.
--alert
Something will not come out right. Nothing else.

Two colours are deliberately absent. There is no warm cream and no terracotta, because that pairing is the house style of every page generated in the last two years. And there is no dark scheme: it was built, looked at, and rejected.

Type

One family in three widths. Condensed sets anything that names something — the wordmark, headings, button labels — because at the same size it reads as a different voice without introducing a second typeface. Mono sets every number, so values line up down a rail whether or not anyone is comparing them.

--sans

IBM Plex Sans

Body copy, labels, descriptions.
--cond

IBM Plex Sans Condensed

Headings, the wordmark, button labels.
--mono

IBM Plex Mono 0123456789

Every value, with font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums so digits keep their column.

Rhythm

Space is not tokenised. It is a rhythm rather than a set of names: these are the steps the stylesheets actually use, and a new rule should land on one of them rather than inventing a value in between.

0.25rem
Inside a value pair.
0.5rem
A field's own padding; the gap between buttons.
0.75rem
Under a heading, before its copy.
1rem
Between paragraphs; a label from its value.
1.25rem
The trim's padding — where the registration marks sit.
1.75rem
Between rail groups.
2rem
The rail's right margin at full width.
2.5rem
Main's top and bottom padding; the stacked rail-to-bed gap.
3rem
The bed's left inset, across the rail's rule.
4rem
Above the footer, and nowhere else.

Layout

Every tool is the same shape, and the shape is the argument: the controls are a rail down the side, and the thing you are about to print, download or export sits on paper beside them under registration marks. A tool that does not fit this shape is worth a conversation before it is worth a new layout.

.press › .rail + .bed

.rail

18rem at 60rem and up, full width below. Capped at 26rem so it never becomes a second column of reading.

.bed

minmax(0, 1fr) — the zero minimum is load-bearing. An implicit auto column cannot shrink below its content, and one wide tile drags the whole document sideways.

.press
The grid. One column, becoming rail + bed at 60rem.
.rail
Controls, and the tool's name and description. Gains a right-hand rule at 60rem.
.bed
The artifact and everything that explains it. Its direct children line up with the paper's edges automatically.
--trim-width
Set on .bed per tool; caps the paper and everything under it to one measure. 30rem on the QR generator, 34rem on the planner, 48.5rem on the sheet.

The artifact

Registration marks appear on exactly one thing per page and nowhere else. They are the page saying this is the object you are about to take away — a second pair anywhere would make them decoration.

.trim › .artifact

Paper stock. Whatever prints, downloads or exports goes here, and nothing else does.

.trim
1.25rem of bleed carrying the four registration marks. The mark is one SVG in --regmark, placed at each corner.
.artifact
The paper: white, a hairline edge, and one shadow lifting it off the bench.
.trim-fit
For an artifact with an intrinsic size — a canvas — so the marks land on its real rectangle rather than on the column.
.on-stock
Text sitting on paper rather than on the bench.

Rail controls

A control is a label on the left and its value on the right, with the values aligned down the rail in a mono column. Inputs have no boxes: a number on the rail looks like the number it is, and only shows an edge when you are pointing at it or typing in it.

These are shown inside a real .rail, because that is what scopes them — the same markup out on the bed gets browser-default form controls.

Actions

One button per page is filled with ink; everything else is an outline. The fill is ink rather than cyan on purpose — cyan is spoken for, and the artifact has to stay the brightest thing on the bench.

.btn.btn-go
The thing the page exists to do. One per page.
.btn
Everything else. Hover picks up cyan.
.btn:disabled
Nothing to act on yet. Dimmed rather than hidden, so the action is still findable.
.seg
Two to four options, all worth showing. Beyond four, it is a select.

Labels say what happens, in the imperative, and keep the same words through the whole flow. Download PNG, not Export; Print the sheet, not Submit. No arrows appended to the text.

Readouts

What the artifact turned out to be, and anything that went wrong making it. Both sit on the bench under the paper, never on it — the paper only carries what will be printed.

.notice

At 24 000 rpm in aluminium this tool is above its recommended surface speed. 18 500 rpm keeps the same chipload.

.notice.notice-alert

This payload is too long for correction level H. Drop to Q, or shorten the text.

A notice says what happened and what to do about it. It does not apologise, and it is never vague about which control to reach for.

.num

Mono with tabular figures, for a number inside a sentence: surface speed is π × diameter × rpm ÷ 12, which is how the planner arrives at 1 280 mm/min.

Text

One prose treatment, used by the explanatory sections under an artifact and by any plain rendered page. Measure is capped at 68 characters; headings take a rule rather than extra weight.

A heading inside prose

Links are cyan and underlined — like this one — because on a page this quiet, colour alone is not enough to mark them. Inline code sits in a recess of the bench.

  • An en dash rather than a bullet glyph: it sits on the baseline with the text instead of floating above it.
  • Used for points that are a list, not a sequence. If they are steps, number them; if they are not, do not.
  • Short enough to read as a scan.

Acronyms

Machining and printing are dense with them. Marking one up means the expansion travels with the page and a screen reader announces it, instead of the copy stopping to spell it out: stepdown is the DOC, stepover is the WOC, and both follow from SFM and the ECC budget a code was given.

.prose
Body copy. 68ch measure, 1rem between blocks.
.prose h3
A subheading. Same family and weight as an h2, a step smaller and without the rule — the rule is what says new section.
.bullets
An unordered list. Works inside prose or on its own.
abbr[title]
Dotted underline, never dashed — a dashed underline reads as a spelling error.

Utilities

.hidden
Gone from the page. display: none !important, for scripts that toggle a notice on and off.
.sr-only
Present to a screen reader, absent on screen — for a label whose control is already obvious by position or by swatch. This sentence is in the page but not on it.
.no-print

Where a style belongs

There is one shared stylesheet and one file per tool. The split is not by component type or by layer — it is by how many pages use the thing.

/css/style.css
Tokens, the reset, the site chrome, and every primitive on this page. Loaded by every page on the site. Anything added here has to appear above in the same commit.
/tools/<name>/<name>.css
What only that tool has: the QR paper, the sheet grid and its print rules, the recipe table, the example picker, the canvas. Loaded by that page alone, through the layout's head block.

Moving something up

When a second tool wants a component that lives in one tool's file, it moves into style.css and onto this page in the same commit — never copied. Two copies of a button diverge within a month, and the design system stops being true the first time they do.

What stays down

Geometry that was measured into place stays with its tool. The sheet's grid, its container-query clamp and its @media print block look generic and are not: every rule in them is load-bearing for one specific page coming out on one sheet of paper.
