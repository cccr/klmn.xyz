# Press bed — visual direction for klmn.xyz

**Status:** adopted 2026-09-20. Applies to every page.
**Why it exists:** the previous look was the SaaS-card kit — identical
`rgba(255,255,255,.7)` panels, one `border-radius: 1rem` on everything
regardless of hierarchy, the same soft shadow under each, on a slate
gradient. Nothing about it said QR codes, millimetres or printing. It would
have suited a billing dashboard equally well, which is the problem.

## The subject

Four tools, all the same shape: **a console and an artifact.**

| Tool | Console | Artifact |
|---|---|---|
| qr | encoding, size, colour | the code you export |
| mass-qr | margin, gutter, grid | the sheet you print |
| milling | material, tool, machine | the recipe you cut to |
| wiggler | wiggle, frames, stroke | the canvas you export |

Every one of them ends in something physical: a printed page, a PNG, a GIF,
a toolpath. The audience makes things. The vernacular is the press and the
workshop, not software.

## The concept

**The page is a press bed.** The artifact sits on paper stock, lit like it
is on a light table, with registration marks at its trim. The controls live
on a dark rail down the left — the console beside the press. Contrast does
the work that boxes used to: bright artifact, dark everything else. No
cards, and the only shadow on the page is the one lifting the artifact off
the bed.

## Tokens

### Colour

| Token | Value | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `--bed` | `#2E3A34` | page background — press green-grey |
| `--bed-deep` | `#253029` | the rail |
| `--bed-rule` | `#3F4E46` | hairlines on the bed |
| `--stock` | `#FBFAF7` | paper — the artifact's surface |
| `--stock-edge` | `#E4E2DA` | rules and cell outlines on paper |
| `--ink` | `#14181A` | text on paper |
| `--ink-soft` | `#5C6360` | secondary text on paper |
| `--on-bed` | `#E7E9E4` | text on the rail |
| `--on-bed-soft` | `#93A099` | labels on the rail |
| `--reg` | `#2FB8BC` | registration cyan — live values, on dark |
| `--reg-ink` | `#0E7C80` | the same accent where it sits on paper |
| `--alert` | `#B23A1F` | errors only |

Two of these earn their place rather than being picked for looks:

- **`--bed` is a hue, not a tinted black.** Press-green-grey is the colour of
  machine tooling. `#111`-with-a-tint standing in for black is one of the
  commonest generated-design tells and this avoids it.
- **The accent is registration cyan.** On pages whose entire subject is
  black-and-white printed codes, cyan is the one colour that can never be
  confused with the ink — it is the plate-registration colour, the one that
  does not print. It appears on the live value and the trim marks. Nowhere
  else.

Explicitly not used: warm cream `#F4F1EA` with a terracotta `#D97757`
accent, and near-black with acid green. Both are defaults, not choices.

### Type

**IBM Plex.** Industrial, drawn for technical documentation, and not the
Inter/Space Grotesk pairing that every generated page reaches for.

- **IBM Plex Sans Condensed**, 600–700 — tool titles and rail section heads.
  Condensed reads as job-press type and buys width back on a narrow rail.
- **IBM Plex Sans**, 400–500 — prose.
- **IBM Plex Mono**, 400–500 — every number and unit. Tabular figures, so
  values line up down the rail in a column. This is functional: the tools are
  full of quantities that must be comparable at a glance.

Scale: 13 / 15 / 18 / 24 / 34, tight tracking on the condensed display sizes
only. Prose under 72 characters.

### Structure

Structural devices carry information here, or they don't appear:

- **Registration marks** sit at the four corners of the artifact's trim, and
  only there. They mark the thing you are going to export or print.
- **Ruler ticks** run down the rail beside any control that is a physical
  measurement — margin, gutter, padding, diameter. They say "this is
  millimetres."
- **Hairline rules** separate rail sections. No boxes, no fills.

## Deliberately avoided

Checked against the brief before building, and these were cut:

- **ALL-CAPS tracked-out section labels on the rail.** Reached for it, cut
  it — it is the single commonest template tell. Sentence case with a
  hairline rule instead.
- **`Version 3 · 29×29 modules · ECC H · 296×296 px`.** Middle-dot meta
  strings are a tell. It becomes a small mono spec table where the labels
  and values actually align.
- **`L — 7%`, `4 — spec minimum`.** "WORD — fragment" with a spaced em dash
  is a tell, and it made every select a different width. Value and note go
  in separate columns.

## Principles

1. One artifact per page, lit and centred. Everything else is console.
2. Contrast carries hierarchy. No cards, no panel borders, one shadow.
3. Numbers are mono and tabular, and they align in a column.
4. Cyan is reserved for the live value and the trim marks.
5. Motion answers an action — a value committing, a code re-rendering.
   Nothing fades in on scroll.
